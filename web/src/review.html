What this does not prove

  • Public flight data shows gate/on-block arrival, not door-open time (the legal test today), so near-threshold cases are marked BORDERLINE.
  • Causes are rarely public: most rulings say "owed unless the airline proves extraordinary circumstances", which is the legal default.
  • No live claim has reached OWED yet: no 3+ hour delay on a covered route turned up in FlightStats' 3-day window during the runs. The payout path is covered by tests.
  • Older flights: FlightStats only shows about 3 days either side of today. Since evidence pages may no longer set times, an older flight usually comes back "not enough public data" until a licensed history feed is added.
  • Airline respondent addresses are registered by the project owner; a real deployment would verify them with the airline first.
  • Every deployment so far is from a throwaway test key; the owner deployment from Blair's wallet comes later.
  • The scraped sources are fine for a testnet demo; a production version needs a licensed flight-data feed (see FEASIBILITY.md).
  • Airlines are not bound by a DelayProof ruling. US law has no cash delay compensation, so US-only routes are out of scope.

Information, not legal advice. Airlines aren't legally bound by these rulings. Test network only.
